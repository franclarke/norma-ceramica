A mobile web page, phone viewport in portrait, rendered edge to edge with crisp readable Spanish text, no device frame and no browser chrome. It is "Norma · cerámica", the one-page catalog of Norma, a 70-year-old ceramicist and retired art teacher from Quilmes, Argentina. Visitors are people around 70 looking at her pieces on their phone: they want to see each piece, its price, and how to ask for it. There is no cart; they message her on WhatsApp and tell her the piece number.

The page is built as an Argentine school lámina, the glossy printed educational poster sold at stationery shops (like the classic Billiken láminas). A solid title band across the top reads "LÁMINA Nº 1 · CERÁMICAS DE NORMA". Under it, a short first-person note: "Hola, soy Norma. Estas son las piezas que tengo ahora en el taller." Then numbered figures: real photographs of handmade ceramic pieces, each with a big printed number in a solid coloured disc and a caption underneath with the name and price in large bold type: "14 Jarra blanca $ 12.000", "7 Taza celeste $ 8.500", "23 Ensaladera grande $ 15.000", "9 Florero alto $ 11.000". At the bottom, starting the next section, the lámina's back side ("dorso") with the heading "¿Te gustó alguna?" and the text "Escribime al 11 2345-6789 y contame cuál. Cada pieza tiene su numerito, así no nos confundimos."

Visual law: bright white coated paper; flat offset process inks, with a deep ultramarine title band, red number discs and a touch of process yellow, and slight print misregistration; a sturdy, wide grotesque for the captions. Everything very large and high-contrast, made for eyes of 70. No navigation, no cart, no buttons.
