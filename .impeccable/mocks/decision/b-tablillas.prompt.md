A mobile web page, phone viewport in portrait, rendered edge to edge with crisp readable Spanish text, no device frame and no browser chrome. It is "Norma · cerámica", the one-page catalog of Norma, a 70-year-old ceramicist and retired art teacher from Quilmes, Argentina. Visitors are people around 70 looking at her pieces on their phone: they want to see each piece, its price, and how to ask for it. There is no cart; they message her on WhatsApp and tell her the piece number.

The page is built as a ceramicist's wall of glaze test tiles. Each of her pieces hangs on its own fired clay test tile on a nail: a photograph of the piece sits on the glazed upper part of the tile, and on the raw, unglazed clay strip at the bottom its number is pressed into the clay, with the name and price printed beside it in large bold type: "14 Jarra blanca $ 12.000", "7 Taza celeste $ 8.500", "23 Ensaladera grande $ 15.000", "9 Florero alto $ 11.000". At the top, her name and a short first-person note: "Hola, soy Norma. Estas son las piezas que tengo ahora en el taller." At the bottom, starting the next section: "¿Te gustó alguna? Escribime al 11 2345-6789 y contame cuál. Cada pieza tiene su numerito, así no nos confundimos."

Visual law: the ground is one deep fired-glaze colour covering the whole page, a dense glossy cobalt blue, never paper. The tiles are raw stoneware with glazes in celadon, iron red and shino white. Text on the tiles is near-black, text on the cobalt is white. Large and legible for people of 70. No navigation, no cart, no buttons.
