A mobile web page, phone viewport in portrait, rendered edge to edge with crisp readable Spanish text, no device frame and no browser chrome. It is "Norma · cerámica", the one-page catalog of Norma, a 70-year-old ceramicist from Quilmes, Argentina. Visitors around 70 years old want to see each piece, its price and how to ask for it; there is no cart, they message her and say the piece number.

The whole page is a wall glazed in deep glossy cobalt blue (a real fired glaze surface with subtle pooling and crazing, not paper). At the top, "Norma" large in white with "cerámica" smaller beside it, and the note "Hola, soy Norma. Estas son las piezas que tengo ahora en el taller." Then ONE column of large clay glaze test tiles, each hanging from a small nail: the upper part of the tile is glazed (celadon green, iron red, shino white, one per tile) and holds a real photograph of a single handmade ceramic piece inset like a small window; the lower strip is raw unglazed speckled stoneware where the number is pressed deep into the clay, very large, with the name and price beside it in big bold dark type: "14 · Jarra blanca · $ 12.000", then "7 · Taza celeste · $ 8.500" beginning below. The tiles are generous, almost the full width of the phone, slightly irregular hand-cut edges, a soft shadow on the wall. One dominant move: the tiles; everything else is quiet white type on cobalt. No navigation, no cart, no buttons, no icons.
